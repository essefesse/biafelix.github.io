About

Content and growth marketing leader. I grew the audience myself first, then built the systems that let content scale beyond one person.

I started as a creator. I founded 20 e poucos and grew it from zero to 100K+ followers across Instagram and TikTok, plus a podcast that reached #14 on Spotify Brazil's Top Education Podcasts. Nobody handed me a playbook. I learned what makes content grow by testing hooks, formats and distribution on my own account, week after week.

That audience became a business. I monetized it through partnerships with The Walt Disney Company, M·A·C Cosmetics and L'Occitane en Provence, which taught me the other side of the creator economy: how brands evaluate creators, what makes a fit credible, and what turns attention into revenue.

Then I started advising other people. Gen Z audience and communication strategy for Vinte's clients, acquisition for an ed-tech in Geneva, and today Head of Marketing at Aptus, an HR-as-a-Service consultancy, where I run marketing as a one-person team on an AI system I built. Most of that job is writing the editorial systems, playbooks and quality standards that let good content get made without me in the room.

A working belief

Everything else can be written down, taught and scaled. Hooks, formats, cadence, all of it.

The problem

An HR-as-a-Service company with no digital presence, no brand voice, no content operation. The founder had real expertise, but it only ever came out in conversation.

What I saw

The bottleneck was extraction and repeatability. His knowledge had to be pulled out on a schedule, and the quality bar had to live in a document instead of in my head. Otherwise every post would depend on me being in the room.

What I did
  • Built brand foundation first: positioning, voice, editorial pillars
  • Made founder thought leadership a process: structured interviews feed a story bank, then ghostwriting on a fixed cadence
  • Wrote the standard down as playbooks, templates, response and voice guidelines, quality criteria that other people run
  • Built a multi-agent AI system for research, drafting and editorial review, with a mandatory brand-voice reviewer. The CEO now uses it for his own thought leadership
  • Launched Café com Notas, an executive event series with partner brands that now generates active sales conversations
  • Produced season one of the FeitosDe podcast and directed external production teams
What became repeatable

Growing my own audience taught me what works. Aptus forced me to write it down so someone else could execute it. The test I now apply to any system I build: if I disappeared for a month, would the output quality hold?

From zero to 100K+ followers and a Top-100 Spotify show.

Read the Exame feature ↗
The problem

Build a media brand for young Brazilian women from zero. No budget, no audience, no distribution deal.

What I saw

Content for this audience was either aspirational lifestyle or institutional advice. Nobody was talking at eye level. The gap was tone and format, not subject matter.

What I did
  • Positioned on a life stage rather than a niche, so the content never ran out
  • Podcast for depth, short-form for discovery. Every episode was designed to be cut up before it was recorded
  • Comments and DMs set the next week's topics
  • Turned the social community into a 5,000+ subscriber newsletter with a ~50% open rate, and brought it offline with two in-person events
  • Negotiated the partnerships directly, then evolved it into Vinte Media, where a Gen Z finance product gained +50% student adoption
What became repeatable

Position on a life stage, design long-form to be dismantled, and treat the comment section as your research panel. The audience names the next hook before the analytics do.

The problem

Sustain growth across two platforms and several algorithm shifts, for years, without posting daily and without one lucky viral moment carrying everything.

What I saw

Follower count is a lagging indicator. The number that predicts growth is non-follower reach, because it tells you whether the platform is willing to bet on you with strangers.

What I did
  • Optimised for non-follower reach and retention first, followers second
  • Separated idea from packaging, changing one variable per test
  • Republished losing ideas with new packaging instead of abandoning them
  • Built hook libraries and format templates, plus AI tools in daily production
The diagnostic I run on any creator

Reach, non-follower share, retention, engagement, conversion. Fix the first broken level and ignore the rest. Low reach with high retention is a packaging problem. High reach with low retention means the hook promised something the video didn't pay off. Both get blamed on the algorithm.

The problem

Make money from an audience without burning the trust that made it worth anything. Plenty of creators get this wrong once and never recover.

What I saw

What brands are actually paying for is credible context. A partnership underperforms when the creator's normal content and the branded post look like two different people made them.

What I did
  • Only took briefs that fit a story I'd have told anyway
  • Pitched the concept before the deliverable, so format served idea
  • Held branded assets to the same hook and retention standard as organic
  • Owned the full cycle: proposal, negotiation, production, paid amplification, reporting
  • Drove R$100K+ (~US$19K) in TikTok Shop GMV in one year as an affiliate creator, through short-form video
What became repeatable

I've been on both sides of the brief: the creator being evaluated, and the marketer doing the evaluating. So I know what makes a creator ready. A clear audience definition, a proven format brands can plug into, honest benchmarks, and a rate tied to outcomes instead of follower count.

The problem

Open a food business in a small coastal city and make social do real commercial work. Not awareness. People walking through the door on a specific weekend.

What I saw

For a local business, follower count is close to worthless. What moves sales is non-follower reach inside the delivery radius, landing in the window when people actually decide where to eat. Friday evening, not Tuesday morning.

What I did
  • Built the whole operation from zero: identity, voice, visual system, production, paid
  • Timed content and paid spend to the decision window instead of a content calendar
  • Ran Meta Ads end to end: hooks, audiences, budget, CTR, ROAS
  • Reviewed every metric against one question: did this change customer behaviour this week?
What became repeatable

Reach, then the right reach, then acquisition, then customer behaviour, then revenue. A view that can't be traced along that chain is a vanity metric, however big the number. Brands buying creators are buying one specific step in it, so it pays to know which.

06 · Gen Z acquisition

A TikTok strategy and quiz funnel that generated 20,000 qualified leads.

Empowerment Lab · Marketing Communications Manager · Remote (Geneva, Switzerland) · 2022 → 2023

  • 20KQualified leads,
    one funnel

    ed-tech audience

The problem

An early-stage ed-tech had to acquire Gen Z students at a viable cost, in a category where polished institutional advertising gets ignored.

What I saw

This audience converts on peer evidence. The content had to feel like a peer telling a real story, not an institution, and we had to test a lot of it cheaply and drop the weak ones fast.

What I did
  • Built the TikTok strategy end to end and created the content myself, in a peer-style UGC format, with script frameworks and hook libraries
  • Built the quiz infrastructure that captured email and WhatsApp, then followed up on both until leads became enrollment calls
  • Iterated weekly against view-through and conversion, not monthly reports
  • Reported CAC, sign-up rate, CTR, email engagement and lead-to-customer to leadership
What became repeatable

Peer-style content beats polished ads with this audience, but only if the funnel behind it is ready. The content earns the attention; the quiz and the follow-up turn it into enrollments.
